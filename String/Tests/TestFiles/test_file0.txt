The Boston Celtics are one of the most storied and successful franchises in professional sports history. Founded in 1946 as an original member of the Basketball Association of America (which merged to become the NBA in 1949), the Celtics built a culture around team-first play, defensive tenacity, and standard-setting excellence.

With 18 NBA Championships—the most of any franchise in league history—their story spans several iconic eras.

1. The Genesis & The Red Auerbach/Bill Russell Dynasty (1950s–1960s)
The franchise's trajectory changed permanently in 1950 with the hiring of head coach Arnold "Red" Auerbach. Auerbach was an innovator, fast-pacing the game and drafting the league's first Black player, Chuck Cooper, in 1950.

The true dynasty was unlocked in 1956 when Auerbach acquired defensive genius Bill Russell via a draft-day trade. Alongside hall-of-fame guard Bob Cousy and dynamic scorers like Tom Heinsohn and Sam Jones, Russell transformed the team:

1957: The Celtics won their first NBA title in a thrilling double-overtime Game 7 against the St. Louis Hawks.

1959–1966: Boston accomplished an unprecedented feat in North American sports—winning eight consecutive NBA championships.

Historical Firsts: In 1964, Auerbach fielded the NBA's first all-Black starting lineup. When Auerbach retired as coach in 1966, Russell became the league's first Black head coach while continuing to play.

By the time Bill Russell retired in 1969, the Celtics had captured 11 championships in 13 seasons.

2. Transition & The 1970s Success
After Russell's departure, critics questioned if Boston could survive without him. However, Heinsohn transitioned to head coach, and a new core emerged starring center Dave Cowens, guard Jo Jo White, and swingman John Havlicek (a bridge from the Russell era).

1974 & 1976 Titles: The Celtics won two more championships during the decade. Game 5 of the 1976 Finals against the Phoenix Suns—a triple-overtime thriller at the old Boston Garden—is widely considered one of the greatest NBA games ever played.

3. The Larry Bird Era & The Lakers Rivalry (1980s)
In 1978, Red Auerbach (then general manager) drafted Indiana State forward Larry Bird. Bird's arrival in 1979 sparked one of the grandest eras in basketball history.

Joined by forward Kevin McHale and center Robert Parish, the trio became known as the original "Big Three."

Championships (1981, 1984, 1986): The 1985–86 team, which added Bill Walton off the bench, achieved a 67–15 record and is widely regarded as one of the greatest single-season teams in NBA history.

The Lakers Rivalry: The 1980s were defined by the fierce bi-coastal rivalry between Bird's Celtics and Magic Johnson's Los Angeles Lakers. They met in the Finals three times during the decade, captivating audiences and propelling the NBA into global popularity.

4. Heartbreak and Rebuilding (1990s–Mid 2000s)
The late 1980s and 1990s brought profound tragedy and a prolonged drought:

Tragedies: Promising 1986 draft pick Len Bias died two days after being drafted, and all-star Reggie Lewis tragically passed away in 1993.

Lull: Following the retirements of Bird and McHale, Boston struggled through the late 1990s and early 2000s despite bright spots like dynamic scorer Paul Pierce and Antoine Walker.

5. The "New Big Three" & Banner 17 (2007–2013)
In the summer of 2007, General Manager Danny Ainge executed massive trades to pull the franchise back into contention, pairing Paul Pierce with stars Kevin Garnett and Ray Allen.

2008 Championship: Coached by Doc Rivers and anchored by Garnett's defensive intensity, Boston completed a 42-win turnaround, defeating their eternal rival, the LA Lakers, in six games to claim Banner 17.

2010 Finals: The group returned to the Finals in 2010, falling to the Lakers in a Game 7 battle.

6. The "Jays" Era & Banner 18 (2014–Present)
After trading Pierce and Garnett in 2013 to stockpile draft picks, the Celtics undertook a remarkably fast rebuild led by coach Brad Stevens.

Through those draft picks, Boston selected Jaylen Brown (2016) and Jayson Tatum (2017), forming a dominant modern wing duo.

Consistency: With Brown and Tatum leading the way, Boston reached multiple Eastern Conference Finals and advanced to the 2022 NBA Finals.

2024 Championship: Under Head Coach Joe Mazzulla and Executive Brad Stevens, the Celtics added key pieces in Jrue Holiday and Kristaps Porziņģis. Boston dominated the 2023–24 season with 64 regular-season wins and defeated the Dallas Mavericks in the NBA Finals.

With their 18th NBA Championship, Boston broke a tie with the Lakers to regain sole possession of the record for the most titles in league history
